# Machine learning

Separate models for separate questions: occupancy (is a room occupied?), thermal prediction (room
temperature 15/30 min ahead) and energy prediction (a building's hourly electricity a day ahead). Results
with every number are generated by the scripts into `docs/results/occupancy.md`, `thermal.md`, `energy.md`
and `experiments/results/exp02-04_*`; this page describes the method. Key results are summarised in
`docs/FINAL_REPORT.md`.

## Common rules

* **No random splits of time series.** Every evaluation is chronological (or by unseen seeds / unseen
  buildings). Temporal features use only past readings of the same room and the same contiguous recording
  (`ml/features.py`, `ml/online.py`).
* **Inference = training transformations.** Live features are computed by numpy-only classes in
  `ml/online.py`; tests check they equal the training tables to 1e-9 (`tests/test_ml.py`). Writing that test
  found and fixed a pandas 3 bug in the thermal features (missing values propagated through `astype(str)`).
* **Portable models.** Every model is exported to JSON (`ml/portable.py`; logistic/linear, trees, random
  forests, histogram gradient boosting, MLPs) and checked against scikit-learn on the training rows
  (max difference ~1e-13) - the Pi needs numpy only, and a scikit-learn version change cannot silently break
  inference. Reference samples in the file are re-checked at every load.
* **Failure handling.** A missing feature, a wrong feature count or an infinite value raises `ModelError`;
  NaN is imputed (trees/linear: training median) or handled natively (gradient boosting). A model that fails
  to load is reported and not used - no random or default substitute.
* **Artifacts.** `models/registry/<task>/<version>/metadata.json` records features (order, units), data
  file and SHA-256, split, seed, parameters, metrics on every test split, training-feature distribution
  (for drift checks), dependency versions, commit and date; `model_card.md` summarises it.

## A. Occupancy detection (`python -m ml.occupancy`)

Data: UCI Occupancy Detection (real, docs/DATASETS.md).
Splits - protocol B (main, forward in time): train 2-8 Feb, validation 9-10 Feb (model and feature-set
choice), test 11-18 Feb (used once, after refitting the chosen configuration on train + validation).
Protocol A (the paper's): train 4-10 Feb, test 2-4 Feb and 11-18 Feb - reported for comparison only.
Feature sets: `env` (the five UCI columns), `env_time` (+ time of day, weekday), `env_temporal` (+ CO2,
temperature, humidity-ratio and light trends), `nolight_temporal` (no light: in an automated building the
controller switches the lights), `co2`, `co2_temporal`. Models: logistic regression, decision tree, random
forest (the interpretable baseline the project started with), histogram gradient-boosted trees, MLP.
Metrics: precision, recall, F1, balanced accuracy, ROC-AUC, confusion matrix, threshold 0.5.
Also: the legacy models on the same test week, a domain check on the twin (UCI -> twin, twin -> twin,
twin -> UCI) and a learned PIR + CO2 fusion for the simulated rooms compared with the hand-built
estimator. Registered: `occupancy_rf`, `occupancy_best`, `occupancy_co2_temporal`, `occupancy_twin_fusion`.
Since 23 Nov 2026 the learned fusion drives the AI in the simulated rooms and retrains itself while the system
runs (experiment 12 and docs/RETRAINING.md).

## B. Thermal prediction (`python -m ml.thermal`)

Target: air temperature 15 and 30 min ahead, predicted as the change from the current measured value.
Twin evaluation (synthetic, ground truth = simulator air temperature): train seeds 11-16 (normal, hot,
cool, high and low occupancy; MO and schedule controllers), test seeds 21-23 incl. an unseen scenario
(all ACs at 60 %). Inputs known at decision time: measured temperature now and 5/10/15 min ago and its
trends, humidity, CO2 and its trend, PIR, the AC command held over the horizon (mode, set-point gap), AC
sub-meter power, minutes since the AC command changed, light and fan commands, outdoor temperature and
irradiance, time of day, room design data. Baselines: persistence and a grey-box 1R1C model fitted per room
and AC state. Models: ridge, histogram gradient-boosted trees. Real-data check: the same method on the UCI
office's measured temperatures (no HVAC information there). Registered: `thermal_h15`, `thermal_h30`;
`thermal_h30` runs live and its forecasts are scored against the simulator 30 min later.

## C. Energy prediction (`python -m ml.energy`)

Data: BDG2 Panther offices (real). Task: day-ahead hourly electricity; load inputs at least 24 h old (same
hour 1, 2, 7 and 14 days before, 4-week same-hour mean, previous day and previous week means); observed
weather of the target hour (perfect-forecast assumption, as in ASHRAE GEPIII); calendar with US federal
holidays. Target normalised by each building's mean training load. Train Jun 2016 - May 2017; test Jun -
Dec 2017 for the same buildings and for 5 buildings never seen in training. Baselines: seasonal naive (same
hour last week), persistence (same hour yesterday); a weather + calendar only model (the M&V style
baseline) for comparison. Metrics: MAE, RMSE, CV(RMSE), NMBE (ASHRAE Guideline 14). Registered:
`energy_dayahead`. A prediction model does not save energy by itself; savings are measured by the
controller experiments.

## Limitations

The occupancy data is one office in winter in Belgium; the twin-trained models learn the simulator, not a
real building; the real room has no labelled data yet (docs/RETRAINING.md); the energy data is one US site.
